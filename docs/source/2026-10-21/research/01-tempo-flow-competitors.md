I am a solo founder in Israel. I am building Tempo Flow. It is a daily planner and coach for adults with ADHD.

It is agent-first. You talk or type to an AI coach, and the coach does the planning and the data entry for you.

What Tempo Flow does:
- Chat, plus live voice coaching. Voice-to-voice in English, read-aloud in Hebrew.
- Brain dump. You say everything on your mind. It splits that into projects, tasks and a plan for today.
- Notes and pages in Markdown. The AI builds the templates and checklists, so you never build Notion-style templates yourself.
- Memory that keeps your context across days, with automatic tags.
- A small "council" of coach personas, for example a gentle daily planner and a strict coach.
- Study tools, such as flashcards.
- It connects to your other tools.
- Crisis safety. If you are in crisis, it points you to a breathing exercise, your own therapist, or the hotline in your country.
- English and Hebrew, with right-to-left layouts.
- You can bring your own ChatGPT plan or voice provider key, to lower the cost.
- Price: $20 or $50 a month. A $1 trial for 21 days.

I need to explain this to non-technical people, such as a family member who may fund it. They need to understand who Tempo Flow is for, what those people use today, and why they would pay for Tempo Flow instead. I need accurate, sourced facts, and clearly marked estimates.

THE APPS
Sunsama, Akiflow, Motion, Reclaim, Notion (with Notion AI and Notion Agent), Todoist, TickTick, Things, Craft, NotePlan, Amie, Tiimo, Structured, today.ai, Goblin Tools, Saner.ai, Lunatask, Inflow and StudyFetch.

THE NON-APP OPTIONS
A human ADHD coach. A body-doubling service such as Focusmate. A general chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini) used as a planner. A paper planner made for ADHD.

QUESTIONS

1. Price. For each app, give the price per user per month, billed monthly and billed yearly. Include the free tier and the price of any AI add-on. Say which one is the monthly price.

2. Planning model. For each app, say how it plans: time-blocking, automatic scheduling, task lists, or a daily planning ritual.

3. AI. For each app: what does the AI do? Is it agent-first, so it does the work without the user typing, or assistive, so the user types and the AI helps? Does it take voice? Does it remember context across days? Does the user still enter most data by hand?

4. ADHD and executive function. For each app, list features made for ADHD or executive-function needs, and say whether the company markets to people with ADHD.

5. Languages. Which apps support Hebrew? Which support right-to-left layouts?

6. Scale. For each app, give public figures on users, revenue and funding, where they exist.

7. Non-app options. Give the price of a human ADHD coach per session and per month, in the US, the UK and Israel. Give the price of Focusmate. Give the price of ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro and Gemini's paid plan. For each option, say in one line what it does well and what it does not do.

8. Feature table. One row per app. Columns: price, AI does the planning, voice coach, brain dump to plan, memory across days, AI-built notes and templates, study tools, ADHD design, crisis safety, Hebrew. Use yes, no, partial or UNKNOWN.

9. ANALYSIS. Using only the sourced facts above:
   a. Where is Tempo Flow truly different?
   b. Where is it NOT different, because other apps already do the same thing?
   c. Which single app is the closest competitor, and why?
   d. Give one everyday comparison a non-technical person would understand, for example against the cost of a human coach.

10. ANALYSIS. What objection would a non-technical person raise, such as "why not use a free to-do list or ChatGPT?" Give the honest answer, using only sourced facts.

HOW TO ANSWER
- Research window: November 2025 to November 2026. Use older sources only if nothing newer exists. Label them OLDER, with their date.
- Source order: primary sources first. These are official pricing, help and terms pages, company announcements, app store listings and filings. Then major news outlets and named analysts. Treat blogs, forums, reviews and videos as leads only, and label them UNVERIFIED.
- For every figure, give the source name, the date published or checked, the URL, and a short direct quote under 25 words.
- If you cannot find something, write UNKNOWN. Never present a guess as a fact. If you estimate, show the sum and label the result EXTRAPOLATED.
- If a price changed during the window, give the newest price and its date.
- Answer every numbered question under the same number, even when the answer is UNKNOWN. I will compare your answer with other research tools, line by line.
- Writing style: I have dyslexia and use read-aloud. Use short, plain sentences. One idea per sentence. Explain any jargon the first time you use it. Use a table only for side-by-side facts.

OUTPUT
1. Verdict: five sentences at most.
2. Answers, numbered to match the questions.
3. UNKNOWN: everything you could not confirm.
4. Sources: the full list, with dates and URLs.
